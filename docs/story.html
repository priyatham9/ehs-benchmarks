Chapter one

One rate, four verdicts

The same 3.20 is measured against four different groups. It is below average, above average, worst quartile and worst decile, in that order.

The site

One plastics resin plant, 300 employees

NAICS 3252. 600,000 hours worked in CY2025, 10 recordable cases. That is a TRIR of 3.20. The question in the room is always the same: is that good?

Verdict 1

Against the country: better than average

Every establishment that filed a 300A for CY2025 averages 3.49 recordables per 200,000 hours, hours-weighted across 375,635 filings. 3.20 is under it. This is the number that usually gets into the board deck.

Verdict 2

Against manufacturing: worse than average

Narrow to NAICS sector 32 and the aggregate is 2.34 across 20,388 establishments. The same rate is now 37% above its own sector. The plant did not move. The denominator did.

Verdict 3

Against the industry: worst quartile

Among the 478 establishments filing under NAICS 3252, the median rate is 0.57 and 45% reported no recordable case at all. 3.20 ranks at the 76th percentile. Three quarters of the industry did better.

Verdict 4

Against true peers: worst decile

Hold industry and add size. Among the 34 NAICS 3252 establishments in the 250 to 499 employee band, the aggregate is 0.77 and 3.20 ranks at the 93rd percentile. The honest sentence is not "below the national average". It is "near the bottom of our peer group".

Chapter two

The distribution is not a bell

Nearly half of this industry reported no recordable case at all. An average taken over that mass describes almost nobody.

Shape

Nearly half the peer group reported zero

45% of NAICS 3252 establishments recorded no case in the filing year. An average taken over that mass describes almost nobody. The median rate for this industry is 0.57.

Tail

The mean lives in the tail

The hours-weighted aggregate is 1.37 while the median is 0.57. That gap is the long right tail: p90 is 5.56 and p99 is 14.21. Quoting a mean against this shape flatters anyone above the median.

Position

Where 3.20 actually falls

Between p75 at 3.01 and p90 at 5.56. Comfortably inside the worse half of a distribution whose better half is mostly zeros. A low percentile is the good direction here.

Chapter three

Change the peer group, change the answer

Your turn. Move the dropdowns and watch the percentile move with them. If a benchmark can be changed by a menu, the menu is part of the number.

Try it

Move the group, watch the rank

Same rate on the left. Pick a different industry or size band and the percentile moves, sometimes by twenty points. If a benchmark can be moved by a dropdown, the dropdown has to be part of the reported number.

Small n

Watch the count, not only the rank

The readout prints n for the group you chose. Some NAICS and size combinations have fewer than 50 establishments. A 93rd percentile out of 34 is one or two plants away from a different story, and should be reported with the n attached.

Rule

Pick the group before you see your rank

Choose the industry code and size band your site genuinely belongs to, write it down, then compute. Choosing after you have seen four candidate ranks is how a safety number becomes a negotiation.

Chapter four

Size is not a detail

Hold the industry fixed and run the same rate through every published size band. The verdict swings 21 percentile points.

Across bands

The same 3.20, four times

Hold the industry fixed and run the rate through every size band the file reports for NAICS 3252. Four bands have enough filers to publish. The rank swings 21 percentile points across them, and the harshest verdict lands in the band this plant actually sits in.

Why

Small sites report zero more often

In the 20 to 49 band, 63% of NAICS 3252 establishments reported no case. Fewer exposure hours means more true zeros, which drags the whole lower half of the distribution to the floor. Comparing a 300-person plant against that group is not a comparison.

Practice

Benchmark at the establishment, not the enterprise

A corporate roll-up mixes size bands and industries and produces a rate that matches no peer group in the file. Rank each site against its own band, then report the spread across sites.

Chapter five

What this does not establish

The limits of this page, written down before anyone quotes a percentile from it.

Limits, stated up front

  • These are self-reported employer filings. Under-reporting is not visible in this data and is not corrected for anywhere on this page.
  • The dots in the density charts are reconstructed from published percentile breakpoints, not individual establishment records. They show the shape of a distribution. They are not 478 real filings drawn one by one.
  • Percentile rank is interpolated between seven breakpoints with an explicit anchor at the zero mass. It is an approximation of a rank, not a computed order statistic on the full sample.
  • Several NAICS and size combinations have small n. The example group used through this page has n = 34. A rank on 34 establishments is unstable and should never be quoted without the n.
  • A percentile is a position, not a cause. Nothing here says why a rate is high, and nothing here supports a claim that one site is safer than another.
  • The 3.20 example site is an illustrative input, not a real establishment. Every figure it is compared against is real.
What a leader does Monday

Fix the peer group before you fix the rate

  1. For each site, write down its six-digit NAICS code and its employee size band. Do this before anyone computes a percentile.
  2. Re-rank every site against that fixed group. Expect some sites that looked fine against the national average to move into the worst quartile.
  3. Put n next to every percentile in the deck. Any group under about 50 establishments gets a caveat line, not a headline.
  4. Stop reporting a single enterprise-wide rate against a national average. Report the distribution of site ranks instead.
